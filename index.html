Ecological Mechanisms & Findings Analysis

Weeks 1–3: Pioneer Biofilm

Microscopic bacterial film and pioneer calcified tube worms (*Serpulidae*) attach. Worms construct hard calcium carbonate tubes that increase substrate surface roughness.

Weeks 4–8: Colonial Takeover

*Botryllus schlosseri* and *Botrylloides violaceus* colonize rapidly via asexual budding (zooid replication), overgrowing primary tubeworms to form dense encrusting mats.

Weeks 9–16: Stratification

Large solitary tunicates (*Ciona*, *Styela*) grow vertically above colonial mats, filtering higher water column layers and creating 3D canopy complexity.

Sunlight acts as an ecological filter by favoring photosynthetic primary producers over sessile filter-feeding invertebrates:

Direct Sunlight (Top Surface)

Direct solar irradiance enables fast-growing filamentous macroalgae and diatom blooms. Algal filaments quickly blanket primary substrate, smothering young tunicate larvae and inhibiting *Botryllus* settlement.

Shaded Dock Overhangs (Report Baseline)

In shade, macroalgae cannot photosynthesize effectively. Heterotrophic filter feeders (*Botryllus*, *Botrylloides*, tunicates, tubeworms) gain a massive competitive advantage, reaching maximum coverage without algal smothering.

Estuarine hydrodynamics and ocean proximity dictate larval recruitment pools and osmotic stress:

Berkeley Marina (Estuarine Protected)

Warm, sheltered waters with seasonal salinity drops (due to Bay freshwater runoff). Highly conducive to opportunistic invasive colonial tunicates (*Botrylloides violaceus*) which out-compete sensitive native species.

Open Ocean / Bay Mouth (High Salinity)

Full marine salinity (33–35 ppt), cooler water temperatures, and tidal flushing. Higher larval species diversity prevents single-species monocultures, resulting in a higher Shannon Diversity Index ($H'$).

Why & How Marine Fouling Species Migrate Across Ocean & Bay Environments
Vessel Hull Biofouling

Colonial tunicates like Botryllus schlosseri and Botrylloides violaceus attach firmly to commercial and recreational boat hulls. As vessels travel between ports, adult colonies drop viable larvae into new harbors.

Ballast Water Transport

Free-swimming tadpole larvae of tunicates and trochophore larvae of serpulid tube worms are taken into ship ballast tanks and discharged thousands of miles away into estuarine harbors like San Francisco Bay.

Tidal & Planktonic Drift

Natural estuarine currents transport free-floating larvae from oceanic bay entrances inward toward sheltered ports like Berkeley Marina, where calm waters facilitate rapid larval settlement.
